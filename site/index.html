what it does

  • Keeps your Bluetooth headphones connected to your phone while you listen to your Mac, Windows or Linux computer.
  • Switches between computer and phone audio from one simple control.
  • Lives in your Mac’s menu bar, or a desktop window on Windows and Linux. On Android, use the app, ongoing notification buttons or a Quick Settings tile.
  • Listen together: pair up to four phones, each with its own headphones. Output delays can differ.

getting started

  1. Install the companion for your computer. On Mac, complete its audio-output setup.
  2. Choose your phone’s speaker, wired headphones or Bluetooth headphones.
  3. Open PairIt and scan the pairing code on your computer.
  4. Start listening in PairIt, then choose where computer audio plays.

Full setup guide

your audio

Audio stays between your paired devices over an encrypted local Wi-Fi connection. There’s no cloud audio server, account or recording.

The phone apps have a banner while their main screen is open. An eligible dismissible video or image ad may appear during a deliberate connection. Audio begins after it closes; app-opening ads are limited to two per hour. Ads use a separate internet connection; they don’t receive your audio. Read the privacy policy.

What to know before trying it

Pair on a reachable local network, then choose Wi-Fi or Bluetooth on supported devices. Timing varies by headphone and video player; Bluetooth still adds delay for games, live instruments and calls.

During phone interruptions, the updated Mac holds its output. Computer media may keep playing silently; pause it in its player and return through PairIt.

The Mac app and installer are signed and Apple-notarized. Phone ad availability depends on consent and publisher setup. Windows and Linux are available as preview downloads. Linux routes phone playback away from the selected computer output. Windows local speakers may also play unless you select a virtual output.
