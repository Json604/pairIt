Effective date: 2026-11-12

This policy explains how PairIt - AudioRelay handles information when you listen to computer audio on your Android phone or iPhone, alone or with other listeners. Kartikey publishes the app.

## Audio stays between your devices

The phone app receives live audio from the Mac, Windows or Linux computer you pair. The connection is encrypted and authenticated with the computer’s certificate and pairing credentials. The publisher does not receive your audio, and PairIt does not save audio recordings. Audio is temporarily buffered in memory for playback. Operational timing and connection statistics are exchanged with your paired computer to keep playback working; they are not sent to a publisher server.

## Optional phone microphone

Phone microphone mode requires an explicit Start action, microphone permission and acceptance by the paired computer. It sends live microphone audio over the same authenticated local connection while its screen is open. Leaving the screen stops transmission. PairIt does not save recordings; a receiving computer app can record or transmit the input under its own settings. Normal listening and automatic reconnect never start the microphone.

## Information stored on this device

The app stores the paired computer’s local address, authentication credentials, and headphone timing preferences in app-private storage. Android backup and device-transfer rules exclude this data. On iPhone, pairing credentials use this-device-only Keychain storage; preferences use the app’s local settings. Pairing credentials allow access to your shared computer audio; keep the pairing code private. The audio feature does not require an account. Advertising data is described below.

## Nearby discovery and sharing

PairIt uses local-network discovery to list computers on Connect and other PairIt phones on Together. Computer announcements contain a display name, platform and public certificate fingerprint, never the private pairing code. Phone presence and the invitation listener are available while PairIt is open in the foreground on any tab, using a temporary identifier and the device name supplied by the operating system (or a generic fallback). Other devices on the same local network can see these announcements. Connected phones send this display name to the computer; local occupancy checks can return connected-phone names to help users request a group invitation. Display names are not authentication credentials. Discovery alone does not authenticate a new computer. Android and iPhone can request pairing from a nearby Mac; compare the code on both devices and approve on the Mac. Windows and Linux use a QR code or pasted pairing code.

Tapping a nearby phone opens the system share sheet with the public PairIt download link. You choose the recipient and send action. PairIt does not send the link or pairing credentials to that phone automatically. Phones without PairIt do not appear in its nearby list; use Share app and the system’s available sharing options.

## Permissions and device access

Nearby devices access is requested on Android 12 and later only for Bluetooth output. Speaker and wired playback do not need this permission. Local networking connects to the computer you paired, including supported USB network links. A foreground media service and temporary wake/Wi-Fi locks support background playback. Notification permission keeps playback controls visible while listening or paused; this idle notification does not require continuous streaming. Disconnect removes it. Android requests microphone permission only when you choose Start microphone. PairIt does not request camera, contacts, photos, storage or location access on Android. Choose Scan QR code inside PairIt to open Google Play services’ scanner, or paste a pairing code. Scanner images are processed on the phone; Google states that it does not store the images or scan results. PairIt receives only the decoded code and verifies the computer’s pinned certificate before saving and connecting. Pasted pairing codes require confirmation. The scanner needs Google Play services and may need an initial module download; pasting works without it. The Android scanner also sends Google technical device and app information, identifiers, performance and error metrics, configuration information, and scanner usage events for diagnostics and usage analytics. This data is encrypted in transit. Google states that ML Kit does not transfer this listed data to third parties. This scanner processing is separate from advertising consent choices.

On iPhone, local network access discovers and connects to your paired computer. Camera access is used only if you choose the in-app QR scanner; you can paste a pairing code instead. Background audio support keeps an explicitly started audio session working. Phone microphone mode requests microphone permission only when you choose Start microphone. PairIt does not request contacts, photos or location access on iPhone.

## Advertising and privacy choices

Both phone apps are supported by Google AdMob banner ads and an App Open ad. Android keeps its banner visible behind normal dialogs. There are no connection ads. An App Open ad can appear when you open PairIt: on a fresh launch PairIt shows a brief loading screen while it loads (up to 6 seconds, skipped when offline), and when you return after at least 30 seconds away one already loaded may appear. At most one every 3 minutes, never while this phone is playing audio, and ads never interrupt listening. Ads are not shown on the lock screen or during background playback. Banners are hidden during scanning, full-screen ads, privacy-consent changes and while the app is in the background. Ads are separate from the local audio connection. Google does not receive your relayed audio or pairing credentials from this app. The Google Mobile Ads SDK can collect and share your IP address (which can indicate approximate location), app and ad interactions, diagnostic information, and device or account identifiers, including the Android advertising ID where available, for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. This data is encrypted in transit. Google’s policy is available at https://policies.google.com/privacy and its partner-site explanation at https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

Where required, Google’s User Messaging Platform presents consent choices before ads are requested. Revisit available choices in Settings → Ad privacy choices. Your choices and region can affect whether personalized, non-personalized, limited or no ads are served. Android settings let you reset or delete your advertising ID. Advertising identifiers and consent settings are handled separately from pairing data; Forget this computer does not erase data held by Google. Contact Google through its privacy controls about that data. The publisher does not set Google’s retention periods. Builds configured with Google’s designated test ads do not earn advertising revenue. Production-configured builds use the publisher’s ad units, subject to consent and Google’s approval.

The iPhone app requests non-personalized ads, disables the SDK’s publisher first-party ID and permission-based coarse-location collection, and does not ask for App Tracking Transparency authorization or access to IDFA. Non-personalized ads can still use data for measurement, security and ad delivery. IP-derived approximate location may still be processed. iPhone banner sound starts muted and can be changed in Settings. Banners are hidden during scanning, full-screen flows and while the app is in the background.

## Diagnostics and support

Diagnostics contain technical playback and connection information. They remain within your devices unless you choose to copy and share them. A support email is composed in your chosen email app and is sent only when you send it. Pairing credentials and audio recordings are not attached automatically. If you contact support, the publisher receives the email and information you choose to send, uses it to answer your request, and retains it only as needed to handle the request and applicable record-keeping obligations. Do not include your pairing code.

## Removing your information

Choose Settings → Connection & pairing → Forget this computer to stop playback and remove this Android app’s pairing and saved timing preferences. Clearing Android app storage or uninstalling also removes the app’s local data. This does not revoke copies of the same computer pairing code on other devices or remove data stored on the computer. There is no online app account to delete. Stop relay playback with Disconnect in the app or Android notification. A small amount of audio already in a network or output buffer may finish while the remote endpoint is being stopped.

On iPhone, open the computer pairing screen from Settings and choose Forget this computer to remove the stored pairing credential. Do this before uninstalling if you want to remove the Keychain entry, since uninstalling alone may leave it behind.

## Update checks

PairIt periodically checks for available releases while the app is in use. Google Play installations use Google Play’s update service. Other supported builds request a small public release list hosted on GitHub over HTTPS. GitHub receives ordinary web-request information, including your IP address; PairIt does not attach audio, pairing credentials, a device identifier, or your installed version. Version and signing-certificate comparisons happen on your device. The latest available update may be cached locally so the reminder survives an offline launch. Choosing Get update opens the official store or GitHub release page. Downloads and installation remain under your control. GitHub’s privacy statement is at https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement.

## Optional public problem reports

On Android and iPhone, Report a problem sends the text you choose to submit over HTTPS to the publisher's reporting service, which creates a public GitHub issue in Json604/pairIt-download. No GitHub account is required. The form warns that the report is public. Do not include personal information, passwords or pairing codes. You can choose whether to include the app version and operating-system version. No audio, device names, pairing credentials, network addresses or raw logs are attached automatically. A random identifier for this app installation is sent privately to the reporting service to enforce five reports per rolling 24 hours. The service stores only a keyed hash of this identifier; it is not included in public GitHub issues. This is not a hardware serial number or advertising identifier.

Your draft and retry identifier are saved locally. The service temporarily processes your text to send it to GitHub; it stores only a report identifier, content hash, keyed IP-address and installation-identifier hashes, timestamps, status and public issue URL for abuse prevention and duplicate protection. Raw report text and raw IP addresses are not stored in service logs or its database. GitHub stores the published issue under its own privacy policy. Public copies may persist even after removal. Contact support to request removal of a report; clearing app data does not delete a published issue.

## Other services

Google Play, Apple’s App Store, Android, iOS, your device manufacturer and your email provider may process store, device, crash or support-email information under their own policies and your settings. These platform services are separate from this app’s local audio connection.

## Children and changes

The app is a general audio utility and is not designed for children. The publisher does not knowingly collect children’s personal information through the app. If this policy changes, the updated version and effective date will be available here and inside the app.
