PairIt offers computer audio relay, four-phone shared listening, and optional phone microphone forwarding.

## Set up and pair

Install PairIt on your Mac, Windows or Linux computer and phone. Keep them on the same local Wi-Fi. On Mac, finish the one-time audio-output setup and allow system audio capture. On Windows or Linux, choose the output your apps use and start sharing. Updated Linux companions route phone playback away from the selected local output; Computer restores it. Windows local speakers may also play unless you select a virtual output. Open Pair a phone on Mac, Listen together on Windows, or Pair a phone on Linux, then Scan QR code or paste the code in the phone app. Confirm the computer’s name before saving. Keep the code private.

Scanning a valid code or tapping a saved computer verifies it before connecting. A nearby Mac can approve a new request from Android or iPhone after you compare the code on both devices. Connecting never shows an ad. A dismissible ad may appear while the app opens, at most once every 3 minutes and never while this phone is playing audio. Background reconnection does not show an ad.

## Audio relay

Tap your paired computer or scan its QR code to connect. For a new Mac, Android and iPhone can request pairing directly: compare the code on both devices and approve on the Mac. Choose This phone to listen here, Computer to use the computer’s output, or pause to stop this phone’s relay audio. Computer audio can play on your phone/headphones or the computer speakers. Use the output selector in the computer companion or the destination control in the phone app. Switching to computer speakers keeps the pairing and control connection; choose Listen here or phone headphones to return. This destination is shared by all listeners, and paused listeners stay paused. Phone output settings separately select Bluetooth headphones, the phone speaker or wired headphones. If a private output disconnects, playback stops instead of intentionally moving to the speaker. Adjust listening volume on the phone or headphones. The updated Mac audio-output driver supports system volume and mute keys. Older installed drivers can be updated from PairIt Settings.

Headphone play/pause pauses or resumes this phone’s live relay while keeping Computer selected. It does not change the selected source or pause other listeners. On a Mac where PairIt’s headphone controls are allowed (Accessibility permission, only used to send the Play/Pause key), pausing the last listening phone also pauses the Mac’s video or music, and resuming plays it again if that pause silenced it. Otherwise this is a live stream: the computer player can keep progressing while listening is paused, and resuming returns to live audio. On an updated Mac, its volume keys change the listening phone’s own volume, and the Mac shows each phone with its own volume, pause and disconnect controls. Track skipping stays in the computer player. Volume and noise-control gestures remain controlled by your device/headphone manufacturer. On updated Mac and Linux companions, a connected, paused phone holds the private audio output. When the last phone disconnects or times out, the Mac restores its previous output. The computer player may continue silently; pause it manually when needed. Resume in PairIt. Disconnect ends this phone’s live connection but keeps its saved pairing. Choosing Mac speakers only changes the audio destination and keeps connected phones available. Stop sharing or Quit ends the computer’s live sessions and restores its usual output. Forget this computer removes the saved pairing.

## Listen together

Connect the host phone to a computer, then open Together to discover nearby phones with PairIt open in the foreground. Guests can stay on Connect or Settings. Tap a phone to invite it, compare the displayed code, and accept on both phones. A guest verifies and joins the host’s computer without scanning a separate pairing code. Use Share app for people who have not installed PairIt. Keep PairIt in the foreground while accepting an invitation.

The most compatible setup uses one phone per listener. Connect each person’s Bluetooth headphones to their own phone, keep all devices on the same Wi-Fi, pair each phone with the same computer, and start audio on all of them. Up to four phones can listen. Each has its own volume and can pause independently. A slow or disconnected receiver has its own bounded connection.

Two headphones on one phone requires compatible hardware and the phone’s built-in audio-sharing feature. PairIt cannot add this to unsupported devices. On supported iPhones, Apple’s Share Audio works with compatible AirPods/Beats. Use the system output picker to select outputs before starting the relay. The separate-phone setup is the cross-platform option.

While phone output is selected, everyone receives the same stream, but different headphones can have different delays. Only one primary listener controls Mac video compensation. Shared listening is not a guarantee of perfectly synchronized sound across all outputs.

## Background controls

Android playback runs in a foreground media service and continues when the screen is locked or you use another app. The notification offers Pause/Resume, Computer output and Disconnect. Allow notifications to keep these controls available. Disconnect removes it. When playback is stopped, no wake/Wi-Fi lock or audio stream is retained just to show the switch. Android can hide notifications if you turn them off, dismiss them or stop the app. Settings → Background controls explains the controls; Quick Settings also switches sources.

On iPhone, background audio and system media controls support listening with the screen locked. Home/Lock Screen widgets provide Computer/Phone shortcuts. iOS does not provide an always-on Android-style source notification. Calls or output loss may stop playback; resume in PairIt when ready.

## Video timing and connection help

Playback uses the Reliable path. Mac video timing is estimated from the selected output; leave correction at zero initially. Some players need playback reopened after timing changes. Windows and Linux do not provide the Mac driver’s system video compensation. Bluetooth retains physical delay, especially for live audio and games.

Guest Wi-Fi isolation, VPNs and firewalls can block local connections. Connection settings accept a manual computer address while still verifying its paired certificate; clear it to restore discovery. Supported OS network links can also carry the same connection, but a USB cable alone is not a connection. Android-to-Mac USB networking is not generally supported. Android can instead use an explicit per-computer Bluetooth connection with an updated companion; Wi-Fi remains the default. Pair the computer in Android's Bluetooth settings, then choose it under Computer connection. Bluetooth is encrypted and certificate-pinned like Wi-Fi, but has less bandwidth and can add latency.
